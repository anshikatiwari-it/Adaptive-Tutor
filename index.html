What you get

Answers with receipts

Every answer quotes your material and links to the exact slide, page or video timestamp. If the course does not cover a question, it says so instead of guessing.

One library for everything

Slides, textbooks and lecture transcripts are split into units, sorted into topics and concepts, and linked to prerequisites. Figures in slides are read too.

Quizzes that adapt

Multiple choice, short answer and numerical questions, a diagnostic for new students, and timed mock exams on the topics you choose. No question repeats.

A model of what you know

Mastery per topic updates after every answer, so the next question targets your weak spots and the report names likely misconceptions.

Revision and planning

Flashcards from weak topics, a study schedule that accounts for forgetting before your exam, Hindi explanations and read-aloud.

From files to answers

  1. Add your slides, textbook and lecture transcript.
  2. The library splits them into units and tags each one with a topic, concepts and its source location.
  3. Ask questions or take a quiz. Answers cite the source and every response updates your mastery.
  4. Check the dashboard for weak topics, then revise with flashcards and the schedule.

How it works

The design behind the prototype, and where this demo stops and a full backend would begin.

Architecture

Grounding

Each unit keeps its source file and location. A question is matched against units, and the answer is assembled from the best-matching excerpts with citations that open the exact page, slide or timestamp. The answer carries one of three labels: from your course, partly covered (naming the terms the material never mentions), or not in your course material. Anything from outside the course appears only on request, in a dashed box labelled as uncited general knowledge. In this prototype the answer is the excerpt itself, so it is faithful by construction. A full system would retrieve with embeddings plus keyword search, rerank, and have an LLM write the answer constrained to those excerpts.

Learner model

Mastery per topic uses Bayesian knowledge tracing. After each answer, the probability the topic is known is updated from whether the answer was right, allowing for slips and lucky guesses, then nudged up for learning. Guess and slip rates depend on question difficulty, mastery decays on a forgetting curve so topics come due for review, and chat questions add small evidence. New students answer a short intake chat for starting estimates, then take a diagnostic that corrects them. The next question comes from the weakest topic whose prerequisites are in place, at a difficulty matched to the estimate.

Evaluation

The Evaluation tab measures retrieval on a small test set with known source locations and off-material queries, and simulates students to compare adaptive and random selection. For faithfulness and answer relevancy at scale, run the same test set through RAGAS against the LLM-backed system.

    How the estimate works

    Each topic has a probability that you know it. Every quiz answer updates it with Bayesian knowledge tracing, and a wrong answer on an easy question counts for more than a wrong answer on a hard one. Between sessions the estimate decays on a forgetting curve, and the next review is due when your predicted recall drops to 70%. Asking about a topic in chat adds a small amount of evidence. Question selection uses the decayed estimate, so topics you have not seen for a while come back.

    Flashcards for weak topics

    Built from your three lowest-mastery topics. Take the quiz first to personalise them.

    Study schedule

    Ranks topics by weakness and by how much you are likely to have forgotten (recall = e-days/stability; stability grows with mastery).

    Evaluation

    Runs the prototype's real retriever on a team-built test set with known source locations and off-material queries, reports RAGAS-style metrics, then simulates student profiles across several sessions. For reportable LLM-judged numbers, run the same test set through RAGAS using the provided script.
